## Review

I verified the frozen hashes in [PREREG_P0.md](pilot/PREREG_P0.md:36), rebuilt all 294 stored trials from seed 0, and confirmed that all 42 cards have exactly one word edit in each primary error condition. The embedded files in [job_bundle.py](pilot/job_bundle.py:4) match the current harness. The P1 classifier self-test passes. In-memory mock scoring gives 252 `CORRECT` and 42 `FLIPPED`; that mock is predetermined by [run_pilot.py](pilot/run_pilot.py:109) and supplies no evidence about an LLM gate. No GPU result is present.

1. **Matched WER is correct; comparability is not established.** The injector makes each primary edit `1/L` on the same card ([inject.py](pilot/inject.py:32)). But every bystander word is appended at the sentence end, often leaving a conspicuous fragment such as “lock the front door but don’t arm the alarm friday”; negation and function words are deleted inside the request ([inject.py](pilot/inject.py:39), [seed_items.py](pilot/seed_items.py:91)). H1 could therefore measure a dangling-word cue. Some “near-homophones,” including `kitchen→bedroom` and `savings→checking`, are semantic substitutions rather than convincing ASR confusions ([seed_items.py](pilot/seed_items.py:98)). **Cheapest fix after the frozen P0:** hand-place the same one-word insertions in plausible positions *within* the 42 utterances, retaining one edit per card, and rerun that contrast.

2. **The confirm prompt is a legitimate cheap, transcript-only gate, but it is leading.** It explicitly names missing words, extra words, and another person in the room ([run_pilot.py](pilot/run_pilot.py:13)). A fluent negation deletion may leave no visible clue, while a trailing insertion does. One convincing control is the same cards under a generic “ask when uncertain about the action” prompt that does not name error types, compared at the same clean-card confirmation rate. More fundamentally, preregistered H1 compares confirmation rates *within* the confirm condition ([PREREG_P0.md](pilot/PREREG_P0.md:16)); it does not estimate a **gate × type interaction** against the existing no-gate condition.

3. **Forty-two cards are reasonable for screening, not confirmation.** H1’s 25-point cutoff is a net difference of just **11 cards**; the 5-point clean-action tolerance in H3 is about **two cards**. I would want **at least 120 diverse, independently checked cards** for a confirmatory result: with plausible paired-card discordance of 50%, a 25-point difference has an approximate 95% margin of ±20 points at 42 cards and ±12 at 120. The current [analyzer](pilot/analyze.py:6) also bootstraps the two conditions independently despite their shared card IDs. GO requiring H1 and the largely construction-driven H2 in either model, while leaving H3 outside GO, is acceptable only under the preregistration’s explicit **exploratory** label ([PREREG_P0.md](pilot/PREREG_P0.md:20)).

4. **The strongest paper case:** a blinded, diverse matched-WER study finds that different error types cause different *proposed actions*, and that confirmation and reliable speaker attribution have distinct, measurable benefits **at stated confirmation costs**; the pattern then survives ASR with selection yields and failures reported. This would turn “WER misses consequential errors” into an actionable result for speech-to-tool systems. The closest verified overlap is substantial: Salesforce’s [BFCL Audio account](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/bfcl-audio-benchmark/) already evaluates ASR transcripts and permits specific clarifications, and the [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/Downloads/2026) includes its audio benchmark. The linked [earlier MFCL draft](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=8yWECy22Zi) describes a broader 8.2K-task audio-and-vision benchmark; that should not be conflated with the 6.2K-task BFCL Audio description. From the accessible author material, I could **not verify** a matched-WER error-type comparison or confirmation cost *by error type* in the accepted paper; its full text was unavailable to check.

5. **Cheapest next experiment after P0:** On the same 42 cards, rerun the one-word bystander condition with hand-placed, plausible in-sentence insertions and **continue only if H1 remains at least 25 points with clean confirmations at most 25%; otherwise treat the original H1 as a sentence-end artifact**.

One additional **P1 validity risk** is concrete: [acoustic_p1.py](pilot/acoustic_p1.py:53) marks *any* sentence-end insertion as `intended` for `other1w`, without checking the inserted word or that there is exactly one. Calling `classify(ref, hyp, None, "other1w")` with `hyp` equal to the reference plus `"cancel tomorrow"` returns `("intended", 2/L, 2)`. The selected P1 set therefore cannot yet substantiate its advertised matched-error transfer claim. Its type-specific selection can also change which cards enter each ranking ([acoustic_p1.py](pilot/acoustic_p1.py:133)).

**VERDICT: PROCEED — 6/10 for one more week on free compute.** The exact-WER construction holds and the discriminating control is cheap. The present H1 statistic, sentence-end insertion, and P1 acceptance rule are named risks; no model result yet resolves them.
